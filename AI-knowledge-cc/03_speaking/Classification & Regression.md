# Speaking — Classification & Regression

## 30-Second Version

**Duration:** ~30 seconds
**Scenario:** Elevator pitch / Quick introduction in a meeting

"Classification and regression are the two main types of supervised learning. The simple rule: if you're predicting a category — spam or not spam — that's classification. If you're predicting a number — like a house price or temperature — that's regression. Both learn from labeled data, but they use different algorithms, different loss functions, and different ways to measure success. Knowing which one applies to your problem is the first decision in any ML project."

## 60-Second Version

**Duration:** ~60 seconds
**Scenario:** Team meeting / Technical discussion

"Every supervised learning problem falls into one of two categories: classification or regression. The distinction is simple but drives every downstream decision.

Classification is about sorting things into buckets. Is this email spam? Is this transaction fraud? Will this customer churn? The output is discrete — one of a fixed set of classes. We use algorithms like logistic regression, decision trees, or neural networks with softmax outputs, and we evaluate with metrics like precision, recall, and F1-score. A confusion matrix shows us not just accuracy, but exactly which mistakes the model makes.

Regression is about predicting numbers. What will this house sell for? How much energy will the grid need tomorrow? The output is continuous — any value along a spectrum. We use linear regression, gradient boosting, or neural networks, and we evaluate with MAE, MSE, or R² — metrics that tell us how far off our predictions are in real units.

The key insight: many business problems can be framed either way. Customer satisfaction on a 1-5 scale — treat it as regression to preserve the ordering, or as classification to focus on exact category accuracy. The right choice depends on what decision you'll make with the prediction."

## German Summary

> *2-3 Sätze auf Deutsch zum Thema*

Klassifikation und Regression sind die beiden Hauptarten des überwachten Lernens. Bei der Klassifikation sagen wir eine Kategorie voraus — wie Spam oder kein Spam —, bei der Regression einen kontinuierlichen Wert — wie einen Immobilienpreis. Die Wahl zwischen beiden bestimmt, welche Algorithmen, Verlustfunktionen und Evaluierungsmetriken wir verwenden.
